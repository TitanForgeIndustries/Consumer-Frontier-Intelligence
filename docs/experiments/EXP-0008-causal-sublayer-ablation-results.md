# EXP-0008 Results: Causal Sublayer Ablation on Late Qwen3 Layers

## Run

- Questions: 3
- Model: Qwen3-4B-Base, 36 layers
- Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070
- Quantization: 4-bit NF4, double quantization
- Compute: BF16
- Ablation layers: 30, 35, 36
- Components: attention and MLP
- Mode: teacher-forced counterfactual replay
- Free-running ablations: 3 questions for all six ablations

## Aggregate teacher-forced results

| Ablation | Top-1 agreement | Target probability ratio | Target log-probability delta | KL divergence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Layer 30 attention | 97.36% | 0.9866 | -0.0196 | 0.0149 |
| Layer 30 MLP | 97.10% | 0.9942 | -0.0130 | 0.0160 |
| Layer 35 attention | 95.78% | 0.9708 | -0.0547 | 0.0405 |
| Layer 35 MLP | 96.10% | 0.9893 | -0.0221 | 0.0251 |
| Layer 36 attention | 94.70% | 0.9419 | -0.1040 | 0.0837 |
| Layer 36 MLP | 96.61% | 1.0046 | -0.0183 | 0.0561 |

## Free-running results

The same six ablations were regenerated with the same seed as the baseline for each of the three questions.

| Ablation | Free-run answer accuracy | Mean token agreement with baseline |
|---|---:|---:|
| Layer 30 attention | 2/3 | 40.40% |
| Layer 30 MLP | 1/3 | 6.09% |
| Layer 35 attention | 2/3 | 7.28% |
| Layer 35 MLP | 2/3 | 17.75% |
| Layer 36 attention | 1/3 | 22.56% |
| Layer 36 MLP | 2/3 | 5.34% |

The baseline itself was correct on 2/3 pilot questions.

The free-running results show a strong separation between local counterfactual similarity and long-horizon behavioral stability. For example, layer 30 MLP retained 97.3% teacher-forced top-1 agreement but reproduced only 7.8% of baseline tokens on question 1 and produced a different final answer. Layer 35 attention retained 95.8% teacher-forced agreement but produced a different final answer on question 3. Layer 36 attention was the most disruptive tested component by the teacher-forced metrics and also changed the final answer on questions 2 and 3.

## Interpretation

The strongest teacher-forced causal disruption in this pilot comes from layer-36 attention. Its removal produces 94.70% baseline/counterfactual top-1 agreement, a target-probability ratio of 0.9419, and mean KL divergence of 0.0837.

Layer 35 attention is the next most disruptive tested component by the same distributional measures.

Layer 30 attention and MLP, and layer 35 MLP, show comparatively small direct distributional changes under complete zeroing of the selected sublayer.

Layer 36 MLP is unusual. Its raw logit L2 difference is much larger than every other tested ablation (5244.26 mean), while top-1 agreement remains 96.61% and the baseline-token probability ratio is 1.0046. This demonstrates that raw logit distance alone is not a reliable behavioral-importance measure in this setup.

## Critical dynamic result

The free-running results demonstrate that small per-token changes can compound into large trajectory changes.

Therefore:

- high teacher-forced top-1 agreement is not sufficient evidence that a sublayer can be skipped
- matching the baseline token distribution locally does not guarantee matching long-horizon behavior
- a practical CFI controller needs a verification or correction mechanism if it reuses or approximates computation
- latent-state prediction must be evaluated on downstream state and output stability, not just one-step token agreement

This result is directly relevant to the CFI goal of replacing unnecessary repeated computation with predictive internal-state computation.

## Important target-token caveat

The teacher-forced target sequence is the sequence generated by the baseline model. Therefore:

- target probability measures preservation of the baseline model's chosen token distribution
- it is not the probability of the GSM8K ground-truth answer
- the causal measurements primarily characterize trajectory sensitivity and distributional disruption
- they do not by themselves establish preserved task accuracy

Question 2 is a useful example: the baseline model itself produced 120 for a problem whose expected answer was 89. Ablation results for that question therefore measure sensitivity around an already incorrect baseline trajectory.

## Conclusions

1. The late network is not uniformly causally necessary at the sublayer level.
2. Layer-36 attention is a particularly sensitive tested component.
3. Several tested late MLP sublayers show low immediate distributional disruption despite large activation magnitudes.
4. Large late-layer activation magnitude does not map directly to behavioral disruption.
5. Small teacher-forced perturbations can produce substantial free-running divergence.
6. A real conditional-compute mechanism therefore needs a state/output verification criterion rather than a fixed "skip this layer" rule.
7. The results support investigating predictive state reuse and selective correction rather than naïve sublayer skipping.

## Decision

Do not move directly to a production-like conditional skipping implementation.

Run a broader causal map across additional late layers using teacher-forced replay, while keeping a smaller free-running confirmation subset. In parallel, begin EXP-0009 around latent-state prediction and downstream verification.

## Limits

This is a three-question pilot. It is evidence for follow-up experiments, not a general claim about Qwen3.

The zero-output intervention is a strong perturbation. It does not measure the speed or quality of a real approximate or conditionally executed replacement.

The free-running results are sensitive to autoregressive feedback and should not be treated as direct measurements of isolated single-step causal effect.

No claim is made that any tested sublayer is safe to skip in production inference.
